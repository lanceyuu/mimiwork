# Forms of Theorizing & Theory Across Research Approaches

Sources:
- Cornelissen, Höllerer & Seidl (2021), *What Theory Is and Can Be: Forms of Theorizing in
  Organizational Scholarship*, Organization Theory 2(3): 1–19.
- Díaz Andrade, Tarafdar, Davison, Hardin, Techatassanasoontorn, Lowry, Chatterjee & Schwabe
  (2023), *The Importance of Theory at the Information Systems Journal*, ISJ 33(4): 1–10.

**Why this matters:** there is no single mould for "theory." Papers theorize in different ways,
and the criteria for a *contribution* differ by form. Forcing every paper into propositional
("boxes-and-arrows") theory marginalizes legitimate contributions. Match the user's aim to the
right form, and judge it on that form's own terms. The journals' stance is **pluralism**.

## 1. Conceptualization — the foundation of all theorizing

Before any "form," theorizing rests on **conceptualization**: naming and framing the **topic**
(the phenomenon in the world) in terms of a theoretical **resource** (a set of concepts +
relationships + vocabulary). E.g. seeing individual talk (topic) *as* "identity work" (resource);
seeing platform organizations *as* an instance of design theory.

- A topic is "a phenomenon in the real or phenomenological world that exists *separate from, and
  prior to,* our conceptualizations."
- Theorizing = casting the topic "as" an exemplary instance of the resource — abstracting it "up a
  level of generality." This integration is what lets researchers ask theoretically informed
  questions and make inferences. Activities involved: **abstracting, relating, idealizing,
  synthesizing, hypothesizing, formalizing** (Weick).
- *Practical implication for a manuscript:* be explicit and sharp about (a) what your topic is and
  (b) what conceptual resource you are using to theorize it. Fuzziness here reads as "weak theory"
  no matter how good the data.

## 2. The seven forms across three knowledge interests

Three knowledge interests (after Habermas) → seven ideal-typical forms. These are *not* rigid
models; papers may blend or veer between them.

### A. EXPLANATORY interest — reveal underlying mechanisms ("what is / what determines")
Objective/neutral stance; formal-analytical reasoning; medium abstraction. Theory must stay tied
to a topic and progressively sharpen mechanisms.

1. **Propositional theorizing** — identify and elaborate basic contingencies as **propositions**
   that explain a topic; "if–then" arguments / logical associations among concepts. The classic
   mode. *Judge on:* coherence, precision of mechanisms, clarity of concept definitions.
2. **Configurational theorizing** — interrelate **configurations** of multiple concepts/constructs
   that jointly explain an outcome (multifaceted interdependencies, not bivariate relations).
   *Judge on:* whether the configurations capture real complexity coherently.
3. **Process theorizing** — plot the **sequencing of events and outcomes** over time; explain
   *how* something comes about from a temporal view (enabling conditions, generative mechanisms).
   Four styles (Cloutier & Langley): linear stages; parallel (co-evolution/bifurcation); recursive
   (cycles of adaptation/reproduction); conjunctive (continuous emergence — meshwork/assemblage).
   *Judge on:* strength of the process account; constructively complicating current explanations.

### B. INTERPRETIVE interest — re-read meaning ("how is meaning constituted")
Involved stance; interpretive-synthetic reasoning; medium-to-high abstraction. Approaches "what
determines" *indirectly*, as socially constructed.

4. **Perspectival theorizing** — introduce a **new lens or new topic** that re-signifies prior
   theorizing and redirects a line of inquiry (a "perspective piece"). *Challenge:* show both the
   *limits* of prior theory (surface reading) **and** the *promise* of the re-signified, deeper
   reading. *Judge on:* coherence, differentiability from prior readings, generativity.
5. **Meta-theorizing** — turn attention to the **enterprise of theorizing itself**: reflect on
   what theoretical categories signify, root assumptions, "prejudgments," biases (e.g. surveying
   root assumptions about gender/race in a field and re-signifying them). *Judge on:* depth of the
   reflexive synthesis and the new directions it opens.

### C. EMANCIPATORY interest — critique & change ("how things could/should be")
Involved-personal, normative stance; critical-synthetic reasoning. Overtly political: expose
domination/constraints, point toward reform.

6. **Theoretical provocation** — like a perspective piece but with a clear **utopian referent**:
   highlight a problematic societal development (a new topic to embrace) and/or problematize our
   current ways of theorizing, making a case for action/alternative (e.g. theorizing
   "bullshitting" in organizations; precarity; workplace racism). *Judge on:* generative capacity,
   "illocutionary force" — does it make us *see* and *act* differently?
7. **Critical meta-theorizing** — like interpretive meta-theorizing but with an emancipatory edge:
   critically interrogate and synthesize a field's theoretical categories from a value-based
   vantage, drawing on social/political theory (post-colonial, feminist, Foucault, Habermas,
   etc.) to deconstruct and re-signify (e.g. using post-colonial theory to rework "the
   Anthropocene"). *Judge on:* the compelling-ness of the critique and the opening it creates.

> **Pluralism is the point.** Do not apply propositional criteria ("where are the parsimonious
> mechanisms / precise constructs?") to an emancipatory or interpretive paper — its contribution
> is of a different kind (generative capacity, performative potential). When advising a user,
> first identify which form they are (or should be) writing in, then marshal that form's criteria.

## 3. Choosing a form

Ask the user what they fundamentally want their paper to *do*:
- **Explain why/how a relationship holds** → propositional (one mechanism), configurational
  (interacting conditions), or process (over time).
- **Offer a fresh lens / redirect a conversation** → perspectival.
- **Reflect on how the field theorizes** → meta-theorizing.
- **Challenge a state of affairs and argue for change** → theoretical provocation / critical
  meta-theorizing.

Most empirical papers in management/marketing/IS default to **propositional** or
**configurational** explanatory theorizing — but a paper that keeps getting "weak theory"
verdicts is sometimes a *perspectival* or *process* contribution being mis-judged by propositional
standards (or vice-versa). Naming the right form can rescue a contribution.

## 4. Theory across five research approaches (IS / Díaz Andrade et al., 2023)

The *role* theory plays differs by epistemological approach. Useful when a user asks "how should
theory show up given my method?" Theoretical engagement should follow the **sociotechnical
tradition** (social + technical factors) in IS; the general logic transfers to other fields.

| Approach | Logic | Role of theory | What "contribution" looks like |
|----------|-------|----------------|--------------------------------|
| **Deductive** | a priori | Theory **precedes** data; defines constructs, hypotheses, operationalization | Theoretically novel & defensible relationships; context-driven (not gap-spotting) hypotheses that *extend/alter* predicted relationships for a new population/context |
| **Inductive** | particularising | Theory **sensitises** the researcher (open mind ≠ empty head) and guides analysis; abstraction emerges bottom-up | Novel concepts, measures, relationships, propositional logic, **taxonomies, typologies, metaphors**, provocative questions — surfacing the idiosyncrasies of context |
| **Abductive** | a posteriori (infer cause from effect) | Theory is a **frame of reference** constantly recalibrated to fit a *surprising fact* | A new explanation that resolves a puzzling observation existing frameworks can't (see §5) |
| **Design** | "if you want b, do a" (engineering) | Behavioural/other theory **informs** construction principles; design abstracts instance→generic | Novel artefact / construction principles + demonstrated usefulness, abstracted from instance problems to generic problems |
| **Conceptual** | argument | Theory **is** the output; an organising framework, research agenda, or informing logic | Cohesive novel concepts/relationships/typologies/metaphors that inspire future studies |

Key cautions from the editorial:
- **Computationally surfaced patterns are not theories**, and "new patterns that lead to new
  insight" are not automatically theoretical contributions (Miranda et al.). Big data does not
  relieve the obligation to theorize.
- Theorising *after* data collection on secondary data risks constructs/relationships that are not
  theoretically defensible even when statistically significant — and propagates ill-defined
  constructs via "domino effects."
- "To cede theory means to give up legitimacy of knowledge" (Suddaby). But avoid **excessive
  truth claims based on extreme assumptions and partial analysis** (Ghoshal) — theories are
  performative and can mislead practice.

## 5. The abductive engine of new theory

Most genuinely *new* theory is born **abductively**: a researcher meets a **surprising fact** that
does not fit accepted frameworks, and reasons backward to "the best explanation that fits the
surprising facts" (Reichertz), continually recalibrating their theoretical frame of reference
(Steiner Sætre & Van de Ven). Requires (a) solid command of relevant theory *and* (b) an
inquisitive mind willing to see the puzzle with fresh eyes.

When coaching a user toward originality, hunt for their **anomaly**: *"What did you observe that
the dominant explanation can't account for?"* That tension is the seed of a contribution — far
more fertile than "what gap can I fill?" (Gap-spotting yields incremental work; anomaly-chasing
yields theory. See also problematization in `making-contributions-interesting.md`.)
