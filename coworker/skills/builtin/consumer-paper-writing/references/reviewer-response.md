# Responding to JCR Reviewers (the revision round)

JCR decisions arrive as an editor letter + an AE (associate editor) synthesis + 2–3 reviewers.
A "risky but interesting" R&R is the typical path to acceptance — the response letter is where it is
won. The currency is **responsiveness + respect + evidence.**

## 1. Read the decision structure first

- The **editor/AE letter ranks the issues.** The AE's synthesis tells you which concerns are
  *make-or-break* vs minor. Solve the AE's top 2–3 first; everything else is in service of those.
- Reviewers sometimes conflict. When they do, **surface it to the AE** and propose a principled
  resolution rather than trying to satisfy both incompatibly.

## 2. The response-letter architecture

- A short **opening**: thank the team, state the headline changes (new studies, re-framing), and
  signal that the paper is materially stronger.
- Then **point-by-point**, grouped by reviewer (and an AE section). For each comment:
  1. **Quote** the comment verbatim (or tightly paraphrase).
  2. **Respond**: what you did (or, if you disagree, the reasoned case).
  3. **Show**: the exact new text/figure/analysis, quoted, with page numbers.
- Use formatting that makes the change findable (e.g., reviewer comment in one style, your response
  in another, new manuscript text in quotes/italics with location).

## 3. Tone — gracious, specific, non-defensive

- Treat every comment as legitimate even when you disagree; reviewers are the authors you cite.
  Critique the *idea*, credit the *person*.
- Never dismiss. If you push back, do it with evidence and a *why*, and offer a compromise (an added
  analysis, a softened claim, a limitation acknowledged).
- Concede small points generously — it buys credibility for the few places you hold the line.

## 4. Concede vs defend — how to choose

- **Concede / do the work** when the fix is feasible and strengthens the paper (the usual case):
  add the study, the rule-out, the boundary, the re-frame.
- **Defend** only when complying would damage the contribution or is methodologically wrong — and
  then defend with data (a supplementary analysis), not assertion. If you can address the reviewer's
  *underlying concern* a different way, say so and do that.
- **Re-frame** when the comment reveals the contribution was unclear — often the real fix is in the
  front end (see `front-end-and-positioning.md`), not new data.

## 5. The asks JCR reviewers make most (anticipate them)

From the corpus, the recurring revision demands — prepare for these before submitting, and answer
them head-on in revision:
- **Strengthen the mechanism** → add manipulation-of-mediator or a causal chain (the most common
  ask; see `study-architecture-and-evidence.md` §2).
- **Rule out alternative account [Z]** → a dedicated study.
- **Establish boundary conditions** → a theory-predicted moderator.
- **Show it's consequential / not demand effects** → a real-behavior study.
- **Generalizability** → replicate across samples/contexts, add field data, non-WEIRD samples.
- **Pre-registration / robustness** → pre-register new studies; report sensitivity and exclusions.
- **Sharpen the contribution** → re-write the front end to name the overturned assumption.

## 6. Practical mechanics

- Provide a **summary-of-changes table** at the top (comment → change → location) for the AE.
- Keep the manuscript's **tracked changes** clean and aligned to the letter.
- If you ran new studies, **pre-register them** and note it — reviewers reward it.
- Be **complete**: an unanswered comment reads as evasion. Address every point, even briefly.
- Watch the **timeline and word/study scope** the AE allows; don't balloon the paper past what the
  invitation supports — `log` any scope you intentionally defer to a footnote/appendix and say why.
